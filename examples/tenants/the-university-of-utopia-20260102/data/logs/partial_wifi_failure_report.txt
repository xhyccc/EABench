# Partial Wi-Fi Failure Report

**Author:** Javier Garcia  
**Date:** 2025-12-16  
**Department:** IT Department  
**Context:** Technical report on the partial failure of the campus Wi-Fi network on 2025-12-16.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

On December 16, 2025, the campus Wi-Fi network experienced a partial failure, severely disrupting connectivity for students, faculty, and staff. The outage occurred during the peak of final exam preparations, exacerbating frustrations within an already tense campus environment amid the ongoing financial and administrative challenges faced by the university. The IT department immediately launched an investigation to identify the root cause and mitigate the impact.

This report details the following:
- **Nature of the failure**
- **Affected systems and areas**
- **Root cause analysis**
- **Immediate response and mitigation efforts**
- **Recommendations for long-term improvements**

---

## 2. Incident Details

### 2.1 Timeline of Events
- **2025-12-16, 07:45 AM:** Reports began to surface of slow or dropped Wi-Fi connections across campus, particularly in high-usage areas such as the library, residence halls, and academic buildings.
- **2025-12-16, 08:15 AM:** IT Help Desk logged over 150 complaints from students and staff regarding connectivity issues.
- **2025-12-16, 09:00 AM:** Preliminary diagnostics confirmed a partial network outage, with approximately 40% of access points (APs) across campus experiencing degradation or failure.
- **2025-12-16, 10:30 AM:** IT team identified outdated firmware and overloaded network controllers as potential contributors.
- **2025-12-16, 12:00 PM:** Mitigation efforts, including restarting affected controllers and rebalancing network traffic, reduced the scope of the outage. Full restoration was targeted by 5:00 PM.
- **2025-12-16, 04:45 PM:** Network services were fully restored; however, intermittent latency issues persisted in certain areas.

### 2.2 Scope of Impact
- **Affected Users:** Approximately 60% of students and 35% of faculty and staff were directly impacted.
- **Affected Areas:**
  - Library and study areas
  - Residence halls
  - Academic buildings (particularly the Science and Humanities complexes)
  - Outdoor Wi-Fi zones
- **Critical Services Affected:**
  - Learning Management System (LMS) access
  - Online library databases
  - Faculty email and communication tools

---

## 3. Root Cause Analysis

### 3.1 Primary Causes
1. **Outdated Network Hardware:**
   - The university's network infrastructure includes controllers and access points that have not been upgraded since 2015, despite warnings from the IT department in prior years.
   - Aging hardware struggled to handle increased traffic during peak usage periods.
2. **Firmware Vulnerabilities:**
   - Several access points were running outdated firmware versions, which caused compatibility issues and service instability.
3. **Traffic Overload:**
   - A significant surge in network usage due to final exam preparations and streaming of online lectures led to overloaded controllers and network congestion.

### 3.2 Contributing Factors
- **Delayed Infrastructure Investments:** Financial constraints and administrative indecision delayed critical upgrades to the network.
- **Lack of Redundancy:** Insufficient backup systems and failover mechanisms exacerbated the impact of the failure.
- **Inadequate Monitoring Tools:** Limited visibility into network performance hindered early detection and response.

---

## 4. Immediate Response

### 4.1 Actions Taken
1. **Network Diagnostics:**
   - Conducted a comprehensive review of controller logs and access point performance metrics.
2. **Traffic Rebalancing:**
   - Reconfigured network controllers to redistribute traffic and alleviate congestion in high-usage areas.
3. **Firmware Updates:**
   - Applied emergency firmware updates to affected access points where feasible.
4. **Communication with Stakeholders:**
   - Issued campus-wide alerts via SMS and email, providing updates on the outage and estimated restoration timelines.

### 4.2 Results
- By 12:00 PM, the most critical connectivity issues were resolved for 80% of affected users.
- Full restoration of services was achieved by 4:45 PM.
- Residual latency issues persisted in isolated areas but were resolved by the end of the day.

---

## 5. Recommendations for Long-Term Improvements

To prevent future incidents and enhance the resilience of the campus network, the following actions are recommended:

### 5.1 Infrastructure Upgrades
- **Replace Outdated Equipment:** Prioritize the replacement of all network controllers and access points older than five years.
- **Expand Network Capacity:** Increase bandwidth and deploy additional access points to accommodate growing demand.

### 5.2 Proactive Maintenance
- **Regular Firmware Updates:** Implement a quarterly schedule for firmware and software updates across all network hardware.
- **Enhanced Monitoring Tools:** Invest in advanced network monitoring solutions to enable real-time traffic analysis and early detection of issues.

### 5.3 Contingency Planning
- **Redundancy Measures:** Deploy backup systems and failover mechanisms to ensure uninterrupted service during outages.
- **Crisis Communication Protocols:** Establish clear protocols for timely communication with stakeholders during network incidents.

### 5.4 Budget Advocacy
- Collaborate with university leadership to secure funding for critical IT infrastructure investments, emphasizing the direct impact on academic and operational performance.

---

## 6. Conclusion

The partial failure of the campus Wi-Fi network on December 16, 2025, highlighted significant vulnerabilities in the university’s IT infrastructure. While the IT department’s swift response minimized the duration and scope of the outage, this incident underscores the urgent need for proactive investments in network upgrades and maintenance. Addressing these issues promptly will ensure a more reliable and robust network for the university community.
