## Meeting Notes

### 1. Opening Remarks by the Chancellor
- Dr. Harper began by acknowledging the gravity of the financial crisis and the urgency of finding sustainable solutions.
- Emphasized the importance of maintaining transparency while balancing competing priorities (e.g., student affordability, faculty retention, and infrastructure stability).
- Reiterated her commitment to addressing the concerns of students, faculty, and alumni while implementing necessary changes.

### 2. Overview of Financial Crisis and Accounting Discrepancies
- Dr. Chen presented a detailed summary of the university's financial standing:
  - Projected budget shortfall for FY 2025-2026: **$38.7 million**.
  - Recent discovery of accounting discrepancies totaling **$4.2 million**, largely attributed to outdated financial systems and insufficient oversight.
  - Long-term underinvestment in IT infrastructure has compounded operational inefficiencies, with the current server infrastructure operating **6 years past recommended lifespan**.
  - Rising maintenance costs for aging campus facilities have added **$2.8 million** to annual expenses.
- Dr. Chen emphasized the need for immediate action to avoid further deterioration of the university's financial position.

### 3. Proposed Measures for Financial Restructuring

#### Tuition Increase
- Proposal to increase tuition by **15%** over the next two academic years (7.5% per year).
- Estimated additional revenue: **$12 million annually**.
- Concerns raised:
  - **Maria Fernandez (Student Affairs):** Warned of further student protests and declining enrollment if tuition hikes proceed.
  - **Ellen Rivers (Board Representative):** Suggested implementing a sliding scale for tuition increases to ease the burden on low-income students.

#### Department Mergers
- Plan to consolidate smaller departments with overlapping functions:
  - Merge the **Philosophy Department** with the **History Department**.
  - Combine the **Environmental Sciences** and **Biology** departments.
- Projected cost savings: **$3.5 million annually**.
- Concerns raised:
  - **Dr. Amanda Lewis (Humanities):** Highlighted potential loss of academic identity and decreased faculty morale.
  - **Dr. Richard Ahmed (Science):** Questioned the feasibility of merging departments without significant upfront costs for reorganization.

#### Faculty Layoffs
- Proposal to lay off **10-15% of non-tenured faculty** across all departments, focusing on underperforming programs.
- Projected cost savings: **$8 million annually**.
- Concerns raised:
  - **Dr. Lewis and Dr. Ahmed:** Expressed strong opposition, citing potential damage to academic integrity and research output.
  - **Maria Fernandez:** Noted that layoffs could exacerbate tensions and harm the university's reputation.

#### Infrastructure Investments (IT Upgrades)
- Proposed investment of **$5 million** to replace outdated servers and upgrade the university’s network infrastructure.
- Sarah Patel (IT):
  - Warned of catastrophic risks if IT systems are not upgraded immediately.
  - Highlighted potential long-term savings of **$1.2 million annually** in maintenance costs.
- General agreement that IT upgrades are critical but should be balanced against other priorities.

### 4. Discussion of Alternative Proposals
- **Alternative Budget Plan (Proposed by Dr. Ahmed):**
  - Reduce administrative salaries by **20%**, including senior leadership.
  - Freeze all new hires for the next fiscal year.
  - Expand online course offerings to attract non-traditional students.
  - Estimated savings: **$6 million annually**.
  - Reaction: Mixed. Some attendees supported the plan, while others questioned its feasibility and long-term impact.

- **Crowdfunding Campaign Expansion:**
  - Suggested by Maria Fernandez as a way to engage alumni and the local community.
  - Concerns raised about scalability and the modest results of the initial campaign.

- **Partnerships with Local Businesses:**
  - Proposed by Ellen Rivers to generate additional revenue through sponsored research and corporate partnerships.
  - Potential for long-term collaboration but requires upfront negotiation and investment.

### 5. Next Steps and Timeline
- **Immediate Actions:**
  - Conduct a comprehensive review of all departmental budgets to identify additional cost-saving opportunities (Finance Department, deadline: December 20, 2025).
  - Begin drafting a phased implementation plan for IT infrastructure upgrades (IT Department, deadline: January 15, 2026).
  - Hold a follow-up meeting with faculty and student representatives to discuss the restructuring plan (January 7, 2026).

- **Short-Term Goals (Next 3 Months):**
  - Finalize tuition increase proposal and submit for board approval.
  - Engage with external auditors to address accounting discrepancies.
  - Launch a pilot crowdfunding campaign focused on preserving faculty positions.

- **Long-Term Goals (Next 12 Months):**
  - Implement department mergers and monitor progress.
  - Pursue partnerships with local businesses and organizations.
  - Develop a comprehensive financial transparency plan to rebuild trust with stakeholders.

---

## Key Takeaways
- The financial crisis requires immediate and decisive action, but proposed measures have significant trade-offs.
- Faculty layoffs and department mergers are highly contentious and could face substantial pushback from stakeholders.
- IT infrastructure upgrades are critical and broadly supported, but funding is a challenge.
- Alternative proposals, such as administrative pay cuts and expanded online offerings, warrant further exploration.
- Transparency and stakeholder engagement are essential to avoid further erosion of trust.
