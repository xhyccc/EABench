# Leaked Email: Board Discussion on Departmental Mergers

**Date:** December 4, 2025  
**Author:** Unknown  
**Subject:** Potential Departmental Mergers as a Cost-Cutting Measure

---

## Overview
This email, allegedly sent by a board member to other members of the university's board, outlines preliminary discussions on departmental mergers as a means to address the institution's growing budget deficit. The email explores potential options for cost-cutting, the rationale behind specific decisions, and anticipated challenges. The email also highlights concerns about public perception and faculty pushback.

---

### Key Points Discussed in the Email:

1. **Budget Deficit Context:**
   - The university is facing a **$15 million shortfall** for the upcoming fiscal year, exacerbated by declining enrollment, rising operational costs, and outdated financial management practices.
   - Recent discovery of an accounting error has worsened the situation slightly, adding **$750,000** to the projected deficit.

2. **Proposed Solution: Departmental Mergers:**
   - Merging underperforming or overlapping departments is being considered as a means to reduce administrative overhead and optimize resource allocation.
   - Targeted areas for review include:
     - **Humanities Departments:** Combining smaller departments such as Comparative Literature, Philosophy, and Religious Studies into a single "Humanities Studies" department.
     - **Social Sciences:** Possible consolidation of Sociology, Anthropology, and Political Science under a broader "Social Sciences" umbrella.
     - **STEM Fields:** While less likely, discussion of merging smaller engineering programs (e.g., Environmental Engineering with Civil Engineering) has been floated as well.
   - Estimated cost savings: **$6-8 million annually.**

3. **Rationale for Department Mergers:**
   - **Administrative Streamlining:** Reducing the number of department chairs, administrative staff, and overlapping curricula.
   - **Optimized Resource Use:** Allowing for better utilization of shared facilities, labs, and teaching staff.
   - **Alignment with Peer Institutions:** Other universities have implemented similar measures to address financial challenges.

4. **Challenges and Risks:**
   - **Faculty Pushback:** Anticipated resistance from faculty members concerned about job security and departmental identity.
   - **Student Concerns:** Potential backlash from students worried about program quality and reduced course options.
   - **Alumni and Donor Relations:** Risk of alienating alumni and donors, particularly those with strong ties to specific departments.
   - **Public Perception:** With the ongoing student protests and recent faculty resignations, such a move might further damage the administration's reputation.

5. **Next Steps:**
   - Conduct a **departmental performance review** to identify underperforming or redundant programs by January 2026.
   - Form a **task force** to evaluate the potential impact of mergers on students, faculty, and overall university operations.
   - Engage in **stakeholder communication** to mitigate backlash and present the plan as a necessary step for financial stability.

### Excerpts from the Email:

> "As we face one of the most challenging financial periods in the university's history, we must consider bold measures to ensure long-term sustainability. Departmental mergers, while unpopular, have proven effective at peer institutions."

### Implications and Public Response:

- **Faculty and Unions:** Likely to strongly oppose any mergers. Consideration must be given to potential legal challenges and union negotiations.
- **Students:** The growing student movement against tuition hikes may expand to include resistance to department closures or mergers. Clear communication is essential to avoid escalating tensions.
- **Public and Media:** With increasing scrutiny on university governance, the board must carefully craft its messaging to emphasize the necessity and benefits of these changes.

### Conclusion
