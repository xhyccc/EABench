# Chancellor's Meeting Notes

**Date:** December 15, 2025  
**Author:** Emily Chen  
**Context:** Notes from the chancellor's closed-door meeting with department heads regarding faculty cuts.  

---

## Attendees

- **Chancellor:** Dr. Alan Porter
- **Department Heads:** Representatives from the following departments:
  - Science
  - Humanities
  - Engineering
  - Social Sciences
  - Business
  - Arts
- **Finance Department:** Mr. Robert Haines (Chief Financial Officer)
- **HR Representative:** Ms. Lila Morgan

---

## Meeting Agenda

1. **Opening Remarks**  
   - Addressing the financial crisis and the need for immediate action.
   - Acknowledgment of campus unrest and criticism.
2. **Review of Financial Situation**  
   - Budget deficits and accounting discrepancies.
   - Status of alternative proposals and their feasibility.
3. **Proposed Faculty Cuts**  
   - Criteria for identifying affected departments and roles.
   - Projected savings from layoffs.
4. **Discussion of Department Mergers**  
   - Timeline and logistics for potential department consolidations.
5. **Feedback and Concerns from Department Heads**  
   - Open floor for questions, concerns, and suggestions.
6. **Next Steps**  
   - Timeline for decisions and announcements.

---

## Key Points Discussed

### 1. Chancellor's Opening Remarks
- Dr. Porter began the meeting by acknowledging the gravity of the financial crisis.
  - Emphasized the urgency of implementing cost-saving measures.
  - Acknowledged the protests, backlash, and media scrutiny but reiterated the administration’s commitment to resolving the crisis.
- Stressed that the university’s survival depends on difficult but necessary decisions.

### 2. Financial Review by the CFO
- **Budget Deficit:** $48.7 million projected shortfall for the 2025 fiscal year.
- **Accounting Discrepancies:** $2.3 million in unaccounted expenses across several departments.
- **Outdated IT Infrastructure:** Estimated $3 million required for critical updates, further straining the budget.
- **Alternative Proposals:**
  - Alumni fundraising campaigns have raised $1.2 million but remain insufficient.
  - Crowdfunding efforts by students have generated $350,000.
- CFO reiterated that faculty layoffs and department mergers are the most viable options for immediate cost reduction.

### 3. Proposed Faculty Cuts
- **Criteria for Layoffs:**
  - Departments with the lowest enrollment numbers.
  - Faculty with non-tenured or part-time contracts.
  - Programs with overlapping or redundant course offerings.
- **Projected Impact:**
  - 15% reduction in faculty workforce (~120 positions).
  - Estimated savings: $12 million annually.
- Department heads expressed concerns about how layoffs could:
  - Damage the university’s reputation.
  - Reduce student-to-faculty ratios, impacting education quality.
  - Prompt further protests and strikes from faculty and students.

### 4. Department Mergers
- **Proposed Mergers:**
  - Humanities and Social Sciences.
  - Science and Engineering.
  - Arts and Business (limited consolidation of administrative functions).
- **Concerns Raised:**
  - Department heads argued that mergers would disrupt academic programs and alienate students.
  - Implementation timeline (proposed for Fall 2026) deemed unrealistic given the required logistical coordination.

### 5. Feedback and Concerns
- Department heads were given an open floor to voice opinions:
  - **Science Department:** Highlighted the risk of losing research grants due to faculty cuts.
  - **Humanities Department:** Stressed the importance of preserving smaller programs, citing disproportionate impacts on minority faculty.
  - **Engineering Department:** Requested transparency on how merger decisions were made.
  - **Social Sciences:** Called for a pause on layoffs until alternative solutions are fully explored.
  - **Arts Department:** Expressed fears about donor withdrawal if certain programs are discontinued.
- HR Representative, Ms. Lila Morgan:
  - Warned about the legal and reputational risks of large-scale layoffs.
  - Suggested offering early retirement packages as an alternative.

### 6. Next Steps
- Chancellor outlined the following timeline:
  - **December 20, 2025:** Final decision on faculty cuts and affected departments.
  - **January 5, 2026:** Public announcement of restructuring plans.
  - **Spring 2026:** Implementation of initial layoffs and mergers.
- Promised to hold another meeting with department heads before finalizing decisions.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The financial crisis is worsening, and immediate action is required to stabilize the budget.
- Faculty layoffs and department mergers are presented as the most viable short-term solutions, despite strong opposition.
- Department heads remain divided, with many advocating for alternative solutions and expressing concerns about the long-term impact on the university’s reputation and operations.
- The administration’s lack of transparency and communication continues to erode trust among stakeholders.

---

## Post-Meeting Developments

- A **leaked recording** of the meeting revealed heated exchanges between the chancellor and department heads, further escalating campus tensions.
- Protests and sit-ins are expected to intensify as students and faculty react to the proposed measures.
- The administration faces growing pressure from alumni, donors, and media to provide a clear and transparent financial plan.
